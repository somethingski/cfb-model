# RISKS

Known risks and data gaps. Add a row the moment a gap is discovered — never silently
work around one.

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Feature leakage** — a feature encodes information from at or after kickoff (final scores, season-end aggregates, post-game rankings), inflating every downstream metric. | High | Critical — invalidates all results | Phase 4 leakage audit as a hard gate, running against production feature code; poisoned-feature test proving the audit can fail; season-forward splits everywhere; Vegas lines excluded from features by rule. |
| 2 | **CFBD API gaps** — missing games, missing closing lines, missing box-score stats for some seasons or FCS opponents. | High | Moderate — silently biases samples | Coverage assertions at ingestion with explicit per-season row counts; gaps excluded and documented here, never interpolated; `gold/` fixtures pin hand-verified rows. |
| 3 | **Small-sample opponents (FCS)** — teams with few or no rated games distort Elo and feature quality. | High | Moderate | Explicit FCS Elo policy defined in Phase 3 (documented in `DECISIONS.md`). **Resolved in Phase 5** (2026-07-26): the 1,288 FBS-vs-FCS rows are excluded from the model frame, leaving 9,085 games; confirmed by Sean. They still feed Elo and the rolling windows, so they influence features but are never themselves predicted or scored. The exclusion count is printed on every run and recorded in `train_report.json`. |
| 4 | **Calibration drift across seasons** — a model calibrated on one season is miscalibrated on later ones (rule changes, transfer portal, scoring-environment shifts). | Medium | Moderate | Reliability curves reported per season in Phase 6, not pooled; isotonic calibration fit on a dedicated held-out season; drift stated in the model card rather than averaged away. **Sharpened in Phase 5** (2026-07-26): the calibrator is isotonic on the 776 FBS-vs-FBS games of 2022 — thin enough that it can map a whole tail to 0 or 1, which is why the output is clipped to [0.02, 0.98]. It improved validation Brier from 0.20244 to 0.19510 on the season it was fitted on, which is an in-sample figure and must never be quoted as evidence the calibrator generalises; 2023-2025 is where that gets tested. Platt scaling is the Phase 6 robustness comparison. **Occurred, and measured, in Phase 6** (2026-07-26): see #25 — the drift is not season-to-season wobble but a consistent one-directional cost in all three held-out seasons. Per-season reliability curves are in `results/reliability.png` and the per-season table is in `results/results_table.md`. |
| 5 | **API rate limits** — throttling or bans mid-backfill, producing partial data that looks complete. | Medium | Moderate | On-disk response cache in `cache/`; throttled client with backoff; ingestion is resumable and idempotent; row counts verified after every backfill. |
| 6 | **2020 season anomaly** — COVID-shortened, conference-only schedules, uneven game counts. | Certain | Moderate | Treated as a documented data gap, not normalized away. Per-season counts surfaced in Phase 1. **Decided in Phase 5** (2026-07-26): 2020 is **kept**, both as training data and as a forward-CV scoring fold — the anomaly is in schedule composition, not in feature validity. Confirmed by Sean and logged to `DECISIONS.md`. The cost is measured rather than assumed: the 2020 fold scores 0.56725 log loss against the 2019 fold's 0.53061, and both are in `train_report.json`. Phase 6 must report 2020 separately rather than pooling it. |
| 7 | **Over-claiming drift in prose** — READMEs, docstrings, and UI strings drifting toward "beats the market" language. | Medium | High — reputational, and the honest result is the point | `test_no_overclaiming.py` string scan in CI from Phase 6; the claim is fixed as *calibration approaching the de-vigged closing line*. **Built in Phase 6** (2026-07-26): the scan checks `README.md`, `FEATURES.md`, `app/README.md` and every `results/*.md`, requiring each flagged term to appear in a **sentence** carrying a negation or alarm marker — a bare word ban is impossible when the sentence the project most needs is "does not beat the market". Seven poisoned sentences prove it fires and seven honest ones prove it does not fire wrongly. Two limits, stated rather than discovered later: it is sentence-scoped, so a claim spread across two sentences slips through; and governance files and source code are deliberately out of scope, because they are where the rule is written down and identifiers like `model_beats_vegas` are named after the alarm they raise. The plan's human grep is still required and is not replaced by this. |
| 8 | **De-vig / spread sign-convention error** — an inverted sign or a wrong vig removal makes the benchmark wrong while every test still passes. | Medium | Critical — the benchmark is the yardstick | Phase 2 verifies the conversion by hand against a known game held in `gold/`; sign convention documented in `DECISIONS.md` and asserted in tests. The gold fixture holds a matched pair (a home favourite at −20.0 and an away favourite at +30.0) so the convention is pinned in hand-checked data. |
| 9 | **CFBD query-parameter drift** — an unrecognised filter is ignored rather than rejected. The plan's `division=fbs` returns *all* divisions (3,734 rows for 2023 against 910 FBS-involved), and a validation error arrives as HTTP 200 with a message body. | Medium | High — silently wrong spine, in the direction that looks like more data | Discovered and fixed in Phase 1: `/games` is fetched unfiltered and narrowed by a tested pure function; per-season counts are asserted within [700, 1000], which trips immediately on an unfiltered spine; a 200 carrying a `message` key raises instead of being cached. |
| 10 | **Games with no betting line** — 96 of 10,374 FBS-involved games (0.9%) have no line from any provider. Worst seasons: 2015 (38 of 870, 4.4%) and 2024 (16 of 920). | Certain | Moderate — shrinks the benchmark set and could bias it toward well-covered games | Coverage reported per season by `make ingest`. Phase 2 excludes these games from the benchmark explicitly and states the excluded count alongside every comparison. Never filled in. |
| 11 | **Games with no box score** — 5 of 10,374 games have no `game_team_stats` rows (2014, 2016, 2020 ×2, 2024). | Certain | Low | Reported per season. **Resolved in Phase 4** (2026-07-26): such a game still contributes to `off_ppg_roll`/`def_ppg_roll` (the score is known) but is skipped by `off_ypp_roll`, `def_ypp_roll` and `pace_roll`, which read the box score. Never zero-filled. Visible in the store as the 1-row gap between the ppg and ypp null counts, and pinned by an audit sample case selected on exactly that property. |
| 12 | **Cancelled game in the spine** — game 401640992 (App State vs Liberty, 2024 week 5) is stored with `completed = 0` and null scores. | Certain | Low, if respected | Kept in the spine because Phase 3 and 4 need to know the game was scheduled; every query that needs an outcome must filter on `completed = 1`. The quality gate asserts no *completed* game has a null score, so a future regression that flips this flag fails loudly. |
| 13 | **Season 2020 contains spring 2021 games** — two New Mexico State games carry `season = 2020`, `season_type = 'spring_regular'`, and kickoffs in Feb/Mar 2021. | Certain | Moderate — anything ordering by `(season, week)` misplaces them | Reinforces the project rule: order by `start_date`, never by week. `test_start_date_present.py` asserts each kickoff falls in its own season or the following calendar year, so a genuinely mis-stamped game still fails. |
| 14 | **Composite box-score stats are not numeric** — `possessionTime`, `completionAttempts`, `thirdDownEff`, `fourthDownEff`, `totalPenaltiesYards` (~20,700 rows each) are stored with `stat_value` NULL and the string preserved in `stat_raw`. | Certain | Moderate if unnoticed — a naive `AVG(stat_value)` silently drops them | **Resolved in Phase 4** (2026-07-26): only `completionAttempts` is decomposed, by `parse_pass_attempts`, because plays cannot be reconstructed without it (see #21). It reads `stat_raw` and raises on any shape other than two integers separated by a hyphen, so an upstream format change fails the build instead of silently corrupting every rate. The other four stay undecomposed; v1 does not use them. |
| 15 | **No moneylines before 2021** — CFBD returns none for 2014–2020 (5,827 games, 56% of the dataset). Coverage starts in 2021 at 83% of games from Bovada alone, reaching 93% and three books by 2025. | Certain | High — the de-vig benchmark cannot be moneyline-based across the full season range | **Resolved in Phase 2** (2026-07-25): `p_home_devig` is spread-derived for *every* season, including 2021+, so the benchmark's construction never changes at a split boundary; moneylines are kept alongside in `p_home_moneyline` as a sensitivity view only. No games are lost to this. The choice is logged in `DECISIONS.md` and must appear beside every published comparison. Not a gap to fill — no moneyline exists to recover. |
| 16 | **Neutral-site moneyline/spread perspective mismatch** — for some neutral-site games CFBD's Bovada `homeMoneyline`/`awayMoneyline` are assigned to the opposite team from the one the spread is stated against. 31 rows disagree about the favourite by more than 0.25; 28 are neutral-site and 26 postseason. Rate: 8.8% of neutral-site rows (28/320) vs 0.1% of ordinary home games (3/3,612). | Certain | Low as built, High if the moneyline had been primary | Contained by the Phase 2 decision that `p_home_devig` is always spread-derived: the defect reaches only the secondary `p_home_moneyline` column. Rows are kept and counted rather than re-signed (n=31 is too small for outcomes to adjudicate, and guessing on the yardstick is worse than reporting it). Surfaced in the coverage report and pinned by `test_neutral_site_moneyline_mismatch_is_pinned`. **Phase 6 must exclude these rows from the moneyline sensitivity check.** |
| 17 | **Normal-margin tail extrapolation** — `p_home = Φ(-spread/σ)` puts 504 games (4.9%) outside (0.01, 0.99), reaching 0.99995 at the widest spread (−62). Beyond about 3σ the conversion is extrapolating from thin training data: only 319 training games have `|spread| ≥ 37`. | Certain | Moderate — affects log loss far more than Brier | Reported in the sanity block on every build rather than clipped away. The empirical check runs the right way: in those 319 training games the favourite won 318 (99.7%) against the model's ~99%, so the normal model is if anything *under*confident at the tail, and clipping to 0.99 would weaken the benchmark — the over-claim direction. Phase 6 reports Brier and log loss separately and states the tail count beside them. |
| 18 | **Elo cold start in 2014** — every FBS team begins at 1500, so the first season's ratings carry no information about team strength and only converge as games accumulate. 2014 is measurably the worst season for Elo-only predictions (Brier 0.2061 against 0.1753 in 2019). | Certain | Moderate — an Elo feature is weakest exactly where the training data starts | Reported per season by `make elo` rather than pooled away. **Decided in Phase 5** (2026-07-26): 2014 is **kept whole**, no burn-in discarded. Confirmed by Sean and logged to `DECISIONS.md` — the cold start makes the Elo columns noisy, not leaky, and a GBT discounts a noisy feature on its own, whereas dropping the season costs 868 games and dropping its opening weeks adds an arbitrary rule. Phase 6 reports per-season performance, where a 2014 that underperforms the other seasons is the expected shape rather than a surprise. |
| 19 | **All FCS opponents share one rating** — North Dakota State and the weakest team in the FCS are both 1200, so the rating change an FBS team takes from an FCS game is scaled to a fictional average opponent. 1,288 of 10,374 games (12.4%) involve one. | Certain | Moderate | Accepted cost of the confirmed FCS policy (`DECISIONS.md`), whose alternative — tracking ~117 barely-observed FCS teams — trades this for small-sample noise. Contained downstream: FCS games are excluded from the tuning objective and from Phase 5 training, so they influence ratings but are never themselves predicted or scored. Revisit only if Phase 6 finds FBS teams with FCS games systematically mispredicted. |
| 20 | **Elo parameters are fitted and reported on the same training seasons** — K, HFA and regression minimise log loss on 2014-2021, and the headline train Brier (0.1876) is measured on those same games. | Certain | Low as contained, High if quoted wrongly | Three parameters over 5,911 games, so the optimism is small and visible: held-out 2022-2025 scores 0.1939, worse by 0.0063 in the expected direction. The train figure must never be presented as an out-of-sample estimate. Phase 6 reports held-out seasons separately; the parameters are frozen in `elo_params.json` and never re-fitted. |
| 21 | **Plays are reconstructed, not observed** — CFBD stores no play count, so `pace_roll` and both yards-per-play features rest on `rushingAttempts + passAttempts`. That is the standard definition and it matches NCAA's own convention (sack yardage is charged to rushing, so sacks are already counted), but it is a derived quantity and it will not match every published play count: two-point conversion attempts and plays wiped out by penalty are handled differently by different sources. | Certain | Moderate — a systematic offset in a denominator affects every rate feature the same way | The definition is stated in `FEATURES.md`, in the gold worksheet's `method`, and in `DECISIONS.md`, so a comparison against an external source is a check rather than a surprise. `plays_from` returns null rather than a partial sum when either input is missing, and `parse_pass_attempts` raises on an unexpected string shape. Because the offset is systematic it is largely absorbed by the model; a level shift in a feature is not a leakage risk. Revisit only if Phase 6 finds pace behaving strangely. |
| 22 | **FCS games are inside the rolling windows** — a 63-0 win over an FCS opponent enters `off_ppg_roll` on equal footing with a conference game, so a team's rolling stats are inflated for the two or three weeks after one. 1,288 of 10,374 games involve an FCS opponent. | Certain | Moderate — inflates rolling features unevenly across teams, since scheduling an FCS opponent is a choice | Accepted cost of the confirmed inclusion policy (`DECISIONS.md`, 2026-07-26), whose alternative — dropping them — would leave many teams with an empty window in week 2 and turn a real observation into a null. Contained by `fcs_games_in_window_home/away`, which puts the provenance of every rolling value on the row, so Phase 6 can measure the effect instead of arguing about it. FCS games are themselves excluded from Phase 5 training (#3), so they inflate features without ever being predicted. |
| 23 | **The hyperparameter objective is nearly flat** — the 24-point grid spans 0.00531 in mean forward-CV log loss best to worst, and the top five candidates span 0.00054. The chosen point (15 leaves, lr 0.03, min_data_in_leaf 100, feature_fraction 0.9) is not meaningfully better than its neighbours. | Certain | Low as contained, Moderate if quoted as tuning | The winner is a *defensible* setting, not a discovered optimum, and nothing downstream should describe it as tuned-in. Every candidate's score and a per-axis sensitivity table are written to `train_report.json` so the flatness is readable rather than hidden behind a winner. Widening `learning_rate` to `{0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.05, 0.1}` was tried and moved the objective by 0.00009 (`DECISIONS.md`), which is what "flat" means here. The one axis with real shape is `num_leaves`: 15 beats 31 by 0.00164, in the regularisation direction the small sample predicts. |
| 24 | **The final model's round count is borrowed from the folds** — it is fitted for 117 rounds, the mean of the three folds' early-stopping points, because early-stopping it against 2022 would make the validation and calibration season a fitting input too. | Certain | Low | The folds fit on 3,833-5,141 games; the final model fits on 5,911, so its ideal round count is probably slightly higher than theirs and the model is, if anything, mildly *under*-trained. That is the safe direction for a project whose failure mode is over-claiming. Deliberate and logged in `DECISIONS.md`; revisit only if Phase 6 finds the model underconfident across all three test seasons in the same direction. |
| 25 | **Isotonic calibration is fitted on one season, in-sample** — the 776 games of 2022, and the improvement it buys (Brier 0.20244 → 0.19510) is measured on those same games. | Certain | ~~Moderate if quoted wrongly~~ **Realised** | **The risk occurred. Measured in Phase 6** (2026-07-26): on 2,398 held-out games the calibrator *cost* +0.00294 Brier against leaving the model raw (0.19128 vs 0.18834), worse in all three seasons — 2023 +0.0025, 2024 +0.0021, 2025 +0.0041. Platt scaling costs +0.00103, less but still worse than raw. The mechanism is visible, not inferred: isotonic collapses the booster's 2,386 distinct outputs into **62**, with 405 games landing on one value, and on the 367 games the raw model called above 0.85 it predicts 84.4% where 92.9% occurred — underconfident exactly where the steps are widest. The clip to [0.02, 0.98] did its job (15 games at the floor, 50 at the ceiling) and is not the problem. **The shipped model was deliberately not switched to raw**: choosing between variants on test-season scores is selecting a model on the test set, and that decision belongs to a later phase fitted on data Phase 6 may not touch (`DECISIONS.md`). Phase 5's in-sample gain must never be quoted at all now. See also #4. |
| 26 | **The 26-feature model barely improves on a one-parameter Elo baseline** — on held-out seasons Elo-only scores 0.19017 Brier against the shipped calibrated model's 0.19128 (Elo is *better*) and the raw booster's 0.18834 (Elo is worse). Discovered in Phase 6 (2026-07-26). | Certain | Moderate — it bounds what the feature set is worth, and it is the first thing an interviewer will find | Not a defect in the evaluation and not a leak: the Elo baseline's scale is fitted on training seasons only, and both systems are scored on the identical 2,398 games. It says the rolling box-score features (points, yards per play, pace, rest, previous-season win percentage) carry little signal that Elo does not already carry — plausible, since Elo is itself a compression of margin and schedule. Reported in `results/results_table.md` and the model card rather than buried, because the honest reading is a finding about the feature set and a starting point for the next iteration. Revisit by testing feature groups against an Elo-only null on **training** seasons; do not iterate against the 2023-2025 scores. |
